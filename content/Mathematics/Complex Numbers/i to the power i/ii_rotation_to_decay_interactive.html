From rotation to decay

Raising circular motion eit to the power i collapses the orbit into exponential decay e−t sliding straight back toward the origin. The same π/2 of "time" that walks the blue dot up to i walks the red dot down to ii = e−π/2 ≈ 0.2079.

What you're watching

Write the circular-motion parametrisation as eit — at time t you have walked an angle t around the unit circle. Reaching the point i at the top takes exactly t = π/2.

Now raise that whole expression to the power i. Multiplying the exponent by i turns every velocity arrow another 90° — they stop pointing around the circle and point straight inward. Algebraically the parametrisation becomes
(eit)i = ei·i·t = e−t,  real exponential decay along the number line.

So the base i says "travel for π/2 units of time", and the outer i says "do it under decay dynamics instead of rotation". Travelling π/2 under decay lands you at e−π/2 ≈ 0.2079 — and that real number is exactly ii. An imaginary number raised to an imaginary power turns out to be real.
