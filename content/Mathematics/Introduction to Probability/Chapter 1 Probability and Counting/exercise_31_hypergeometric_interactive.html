What's going on

A sequence of numbers is called a hypergeometric series when the ratio between its consecutive terms is a rational function of the index — that is, a polynomial divided by another polynomial. The hypergeometric distribution is the distribution whose probabilities form such a series. The "hyper" doesn't refer to anything exotic; it just marks this rationality property, which generalizes the simpler geometric series (where the ratio is a constant).

So the test for "is this a hypergeometric distribution?" is essentially the test "does $P_{k+1}/P_k$ simplify to a ratio of polynomials in $k$?" — and for the elk problem, the answer is yes, with the exact polynomial fraction shown above.

Why the ratio formula works — algebra in 3 steps

Write $P_k$ and $P_{k+1}$ as quotients of three binomial coefficients (numerator: tagged times untagged; denominator: total). When you form $P_{k+1}/P_k$, the $\binom{N}{m}$ in both denominators cancels, leaving

$\dfrac{P_{k+1}}{P_k} = \dfrac{\binom{n}{k+1}}{\binom{n}{k}} \cdot \dfrac{\binom{N-n}{m-k-1}}{\binom{N-n}{m-k}}$.

Step 1 — Tagged factor. Expand $\binom{n}{k}$ and $\binom{n}{k+1}$ into factorials. The $n!$'s cancel; $k!/(k+1)! = 1/(k+1)$ on the bottom; $(n-k)!/(n-k-1)! = (n-k)$ on top. Result: $\dfrac{n-k}{k+1}$.

Step 2 — Untagged factor. Same move on $\binom{N-n}{m-k}$ and $\binom{N-n}{m-k-1}$. The $(N-n)!$'s cancel; $(m-k)/(m-k-1)! = (m-k)$ on top; $(N-n-m+k+1)$ on bottom. Result: $\dfrac{m-k}{N-n-m+k+1}$.

Step 3 — Multiply. Multiplying the two clean factors gives (n-k)(m-k) / ((k+1)(N-n-m+k+1)). It looked monstrous but it was always just two cancellations stacked.
