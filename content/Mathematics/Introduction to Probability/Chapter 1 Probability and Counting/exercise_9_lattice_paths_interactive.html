Lattice Paths

Right · Up

Standing at the origin of an integer grid, with permission to step only right or up by one unit at a time — how many distinct routes lead to a given destination? Below, the question is settled for two endpoints: (2, 2) and (3, 2). Click any path on the right to trace it.

PROBLEM I to (2, 2)
6
Total paths C(4, 2) = 4! / (2! · 2!) = 6
2 right-steps + 2 up-steps = 4 moves.
Choose which 2 of the 4 are rights.

PROBLEM II to (3, 2)
10
Total paths C(5, 2) = 5! / (2! · 3!) = 10
3 right-steps + 2 up-steps = 5 moves.
Choose which 2 of the 5 are ups.
The ten paths R = right · U = up
— On the formula

Any path from (0, 0) to (m, n) consists of exactly m right-steps and n up-steps. The order in which we take them is the only thing that distinguishes one path from another. Counting the paths is therefore identical to counting the number of ways to arrange m R's and n U's in a sequence.

paths(m, n) = C(m + n, m) = C(m + n, n) = (m + n)! / (m! · n!)

For (2, 2), that's C(4, 2) = 6. For (3, 2), C(5, 2) = 10. The same numbers appear in Pascal's triangle: each entry in the triangle records the count of paths from the top to that position. A nice consequence is that the number of paths to a lattice point equals the sum of the paths to the two points immediately below and to its left.
