What you're looking at

The lattice and Pascal's triangle store the same numbers in different geometries. The number of monotone paths from (0,0) to (a, b) — using only right and up steps — is C(a+b, a). When you arrange these path-counts at every lattice point and rotate the lattice 45° clockwise, the counts line up exactly with Pascal's triangle: anti-diagonal of the lattice (constant a+b) becomes a row of the triangle.

Pascal's identity says C(a+b, a) = C(a+b−1, a−1) + C(a+b−1, a). On the lattice, the predecessors are the points one step left (a−1, b) and one step down (a, b−1) — the only two points from which you can reach (a, b) in one move. In the triangle, those same predecessors are the two entries directly above the selected one. Either picture, the count at the destination is the sum of the counts at its two parents.

Use the Sample paths controls to step through every individual path from (0,0) to your selection — for example, (3,2) has 10 paths, (6,6) has 924, and you can scroll through them all.
