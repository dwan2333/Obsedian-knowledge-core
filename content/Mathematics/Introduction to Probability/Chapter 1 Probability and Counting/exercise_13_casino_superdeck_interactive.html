Exercise 13 · Casino Superdeck

Stars & Bars

A casino mixes 10 standard decks into one giant "superdeck" — 520 cards, with ten copies of each of the 52 cards. Deal a 10-card hand. Order doesn't matter, and copies of the same card are indistinguishable. How many possible hands are there?

The conceptual move

In an ordinary deck the 52 cards are all distinct, so a hand is a subset. But here the ten copies of (say) the Ace of Spades are interchangeable. So holding two Aces of Spades is just holding two Aces of Spades — the two copies aren't tagged.

That changes what a "hand" is. A hand is no longer a subset; it's a multiset — described entirely by how many of each card type you hold:

A hand is a tuple (n₁, n₂, ..., n₅₂) with each nᵢ ≥ 0, each nᵢ ≤ 10, and n₁ + n₂ + ⋯ + n₅₂ = 10.

The upper bound nᵢ ≤ 10 is automatically satisfied — since the total is only 10, no single nᵢ can exceed 10 anyway. So we just count nonnegative integer solutions to that sum, no inclusion-exclusion needed. That's exactly what stars and bars solves.

Apply to the casino

The numerical value
90,177,170,226
≈ 9.02 × 10¹⁰
roughly ninety billion possible hands
— Bose-Einstein

The hint refers to Bose-Einstein statistics, which counts the number of ways to distribute k indistinguishable particles among n distinguishable states. The answer is C(n + k − 1, k) — exactly what we used.

The bridge to physics: photons (the indistinguishable "stars") populating energy levels (the distinguishable "bins") obey this same combinatorial rule, in contrast to Maxwell-Boltzmann (distinguishable particles) or Fermi-Dirac (at most one per state). Bose and Einstein worked this out in 1924; combinatorialists have called it stars and bars for far longer.

One subtlety worth keeping in mind: this problem only works cleanly because k ≤ 10. If the casino dealt 11-card hands, the constraint nᵢ ≤ 10 would start to bite, and you'd subtract the bad configurations via inclusion-exclusion. The fact that the hand size doesn't exceed the per-card supply is what lets stars and bars handle the count directly.
