— The takeaway

For n = 5, the left side counts 1³+2³+3³+4³+5³ = 1+8+27+64+125 = 225 tuples. The right side counts the same tuples by sorting them according to how many distinct numbers they contain — 90 + 120 + 15 = 225.

The 90 tuples with 4 distinct values come from choosing 4 numbers out of {0,...,5} in C(6,4) = 15 ways, then arranging the smaller three in 3! = 6 ways. The 120 tuples with 3 distinct values come from C(6,3) = 20 choices times 6 arrangements (two values, both required to appear, in three slots: 2³−2 = 6). The 15 tuples with 2 distinct values come from C(6,2) = 15 choices times 1 arrangement.

Two stories. Same 225 tuples. The identity isn't a coincidence — it's two correct counts of one collection. And by the side note from problem (a), the LHS also equals (1+2+3+4+5)² = 15² = 225, which is why sum of first n cubes = (sum of first n integers)².
