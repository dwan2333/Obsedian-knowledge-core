# The Brothers Karamazov — Chapter 1

*Lines kept from **Chapter 1**. Part of [The Brothers Karamazov](<The Brothers Karamazov (Main).md>).*

---

> [!quote] A man capable only in worldly affairs (p. 10)
> "But he was one of those senseless persons who are very well capable of looking after their worldly affairs, and, apparently, after nothing else."

> [!quote] If the precipice had been less picturesque (p. 11)
> "Indeed, if this [precipice](<../../../Vocabulary/Words/precipice.md>), a chosen and favorite spot of hers, had been less [picturesque](<../../../Vocabulary/Words/picturesque.md>), if there had been a [prosaic](<../../../Vocabulary/Words/prosaic.md>) flat bank in its place, most likely the suicide would never have taken place."

> [!quote] Action as an echo of others' ideas (p. 11)
> "Adelaida Ivanovna Miusov's action was similarly, no doubt, an echo of other people's ideas, and was due to the irritation caused by lack of mental freedom. She wanted, perhaps, to show her feminine independence, to override class distinctions and the [despotism](<../../../Vocabulary/Words/despotism.md>) of her family. And a [pliable](<../../../Vocabulary/Words/pliable.md>) imagination persuaded her, we must suppose, for a brief moment, that Fyodor Pavlovitch, in spite of his parasitic position, was one of the bold and ironical spirits of that progressive [epoch](<../../../Vocabulary/Words/epoch.md>), though he was, in fact, an ill-natured buffoon and nothing more. What gave the marriage [piquancy](<../../../Vocabulary/Words/piquancy.md>) was that it was preceded by an [elopement](<../../../Vocabulary/Words/elopement.md>), and"

> [!quote] What gave the marriage its piquancy (p. 11)
> "What gave the marriage [piquancy](<../../../Vocabulary/Words/piquancy.md>) was that it was preceded by an [elopement](<../../../Vocabulary/Words/elopement.md>), and this greatly captivated Adelaide Ivanovna's fancy."

> [!quote] Worn down to consent (p. 13)
> "He would probably have succeeded merely from her moral fatigue and desire to get rid of him, and from the contempt that was induced in her also by his persistent, shameless [importunity](<../../../Vocabulary/Words/importunity.md>)."

> [!quote] To parade his woes (p. 14)
> "To parade his woes with embellishments."
