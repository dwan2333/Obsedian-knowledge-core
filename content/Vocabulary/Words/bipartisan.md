# bipartisan

> [!definition] /ˌbaɪˈpɑːtɪzæn/ (by-PAR-tih-zan) · *adjective*
> Supported by or involving two political parties, especially the two main parties that usually oppose each other.

> [!example] In a sentence
> - The infrastructure bill passed with **bipartisan** support, a rare sight in a divided Congress.
> - A **bipartisan** group of senators wrote to the regulator demanding an inquiry.
> - Sanctions on the regime are one of the few issues with **bipartisan** agreement.

> [!info] Register & connotation
> Neutral to approving; a staple of US political news (*bipartisan bill, bipartisan support, bipartisan deal, bipartisan consensus*). It signals cooperation across the usual divide. In countries with many parties, *cross-party* is the usual word. Its opposite pole is *partisan*: strongly, often blindly, loyal to one side. *Nonpartisan* means tied to no party at all. From *bi-*, "two", plus *partisan*, from Italian *partigiano*, from *parte*, "part, side".
