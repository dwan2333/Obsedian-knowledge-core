# trample

> [!definition] /ˈtræmpəl/ · *verb*
> To tread heavily on something, crushing or damaging it underfoot. Figuratively, to crush or ride roughshod over — to *trample on* someone's rights, feelings, or dignity is to oppress them without care.

> [!example] In a sentence
> - The stampeding cattle **trampled** the fence flat and poured into the road.
> - She would not stand by and watch them **trample** the rights of the powerless.
> - In his haste he **trampled** the flowerbeds he had spent all spring tending.

> [!info] Register & connotation
> Everyday and vivid, with real force. The figurative sense carries indignation — it casts the wrongdoer as careless and brutal. Stronger and more physical than *ignore* or *override*; use it when you want the reader to feel something being ground down.
