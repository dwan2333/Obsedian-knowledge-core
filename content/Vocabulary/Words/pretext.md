# pretext

> [!definition] /ˈpriːtɛkst/ · *noun*
> A false reason given to hide the real one; a pretended justification for an action.

> [!example] In a sentence
> - He called on some **pretext** about returning a book, but really he wanted to see her.
> - The border incident was a mere **pretext** for the invasion already planned.
> - She left early on the **pretext** of a headache.

> [!info] Register & connotation
> Formal-ish and negative — it exposes a stated reason as a cover for the true motive. The language of diplomacy, intrigue, and quiet deception; a cousin of the cunning in [artfulness](artfulness.md). Close to "excuse," but always with the implication of concealment.
