# nefarious

> [!definition] /nɪˈfɛəriəs/ (nih-FAIR-ee-us) · *adjective*
> Wicked or criminal — said mostly of an activity, plan, or purpose rather than of a person's character.

> [!example] In a sentence
> - The audit uncovered a **nefarious** scheme to skim money from the employees' pension fund.
> - Stolen passwords are sold online for all kinds of **nefarious** purposes.
> - My cat clearly has **nefarious** plans for the glass on the edge of the table.

> [!info] Register & connotation
> Formal and strongly negative. At home in journalism and security writing (*nefarious actors*, *nefarious activities*), and often used tongue-in-cheek for mock villainy, as with the cat. More formal than *shady* or *wicked*, and less visceral than [vileness](vileness.md). From Latin *nefas*, "a crime, a wrong against divine law" (*ne-* "not" + *fas* "what is right").
