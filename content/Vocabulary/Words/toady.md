# toady

> [!definition] /ˈtoʊdi/ · *noun (also verb)*
> A person who flatters and fawns over the powerful to win favour; a sycophant. As a verb, to behave in this servile, ingratiating way — *to toady to the boss*.

> [!example] In a sentence
> - The minister was surrounded by **toadies** who agreed with every word he said and meant none of it.
> - She refused to **toady** to the examiners, and her blunt honesty nearly cost her the post.
> - Every court has its **toady** — the one who laughs hardest at the king's dullest jokes.

> [!info] Register & connotation
> Informal and witheringly contemptuous. A *toady* is never admired; the word passes judgment on the flatterer's spinelessness. Slightly old-fashioned but still vivid — close cousins are *bootlicker* and *yes-man*.
