# espionage

> [!definition] /ˈɛspiənɑːʒ/ (ES-pee-uh-nahzh) · *noun*
> Spying: secretly gathering information, especially a government's political or military secrets. *Industrial* or *corporate espionage* is the same thing aimed at a company's trade secrets.

> [!example] In a sentence
> - He was arrested on charges of **espionage** after passing military secrets to a foreign government.
> - The company sued a former engineer for industrial **espionage**, claiming he had copied its chip designs before leaving.
> - Cold War novels are full of **espionage**: double agents, coded messages, and dead drops in public parks.

> [!info] Register & connotation
> Formal and neutral in tone, common in news, law, and history (*charged with espionage*, *an espionage ring*, *cyber espionage*). It names the activity; the person is a *spy* or, more formally, an *agent*. Plainer: *spying*. From French *espionnage*, from *espion*, "spy".
