# salient

> [!definition] /ˈseɪliənt/ · *adjective (also noun)*
> Most noticeable or most important; standing out from the rest (*the salient points*, *a salient feature*). As a military noun, a part of a battle line that bulges forward into enemy territory. The noun for the quality is *salience*.

> [!example] In a sentence
> - The report's **salient** points fit on a single page.
> - Her most **salient** quality as a manager was her calm under pressure.
> - The army held a narrow **salient** that was exposed on three sides.

> [!info] Register & connotation
> Formal, at home in reports, essays, and analysis; in conversation *main* or *key* is plainer. From Latin *salire*, "to leap": a salient point is one that leaps out at you. In machine learning, a **saliency map** shows which pixels of an image a model relied on most for its prediction.
