# emancipation

> [!definition] /ɪˌmænsɪˈpeɪʃən/ · *noun*
> The act or process of being set free from legal, social, or political restraint — above all from slavery, but also from any controlling power or constraint.

> [!example] In a sentence
> - The **emancipation** of the enslaved people was proclaimed, but real freedom came slowly and unevenly.
> - She wrote about the **emancipation** of women from laws that had treated them as property.
> - For him, leaving the family firm was a kind of personal **emancipation**.

> [!info] Register & connotation
> Formal, weighty, and strongly positive — a word of liberation and dignity, common in history and political writing. From Latin roots meaning roughly "to release from a hand's grasp." The opposing force is [despotism](despotism.md).
