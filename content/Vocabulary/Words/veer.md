# veer

> [!definition] /vɪə/ (VEER) · *verb*
> To change direction suddenly. Figuratively, (of talk, policy, opinion, or prices) to shift suddenly from one course to another.

> [!example] In a sentence
> - The truck **veered** across two lanes to avoid a deer.
> - Halfway through the interview, the conversation **veered** into politics.
> - The party has **veered** sharply to the right since the election.

> [!info] Register & connotation
> Everyday, neutral, and vivid. It implies a change that is sudden and often unplanned or out of control. It usually comes with a direction: *veer off course, veer away from, veer towards, veer left, veer into*. A *swerve* is a sharp turn made on purpose to avoid something, a *drift* is slow, and a *lurch* is abrupt and clumsy. From French *virer*, "to turn".
