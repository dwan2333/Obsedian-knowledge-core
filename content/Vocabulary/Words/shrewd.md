# shrewd

> [!definition] /ʃruːd/ · *adjective*
> Having sharp, practical judgment; quick to see what is to one's own advantage and to act on it. Clever in a worldly, calculating way rather than a bookish one.

> [!example] In a sentence
> - A **shrewd** negotiator, she let the other side name a price first and then quietly halved it.
> - He made a **shrewd** guess that the company would be sold within the year, and bought the stock.
> - Her grandmother had a **shrewd** eye for character and was rarely fooled by charm.

> [!info] Register & connotation
> Neutral-to-positive and broadly usable. *Shrewd* admires practical intelligence, but with a faint edge of self-interest — it sits between the warm *wise* and the cold *cunning*. Call someone shrewd and you credit their judgment without quite calling them kind.
