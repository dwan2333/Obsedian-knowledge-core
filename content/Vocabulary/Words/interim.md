# interim

> [!definition] /ˈɪntərɪm/ (IN-tuh-rim) · *adjective (also noun)*
> Serving or lasting for a short time until something permanent takes over (*an interim CEO*, *an interim government*). As a noun, the time between two events: *in the interim* means meanwhile. In finance, covering part of a year, before the final annual figure: *interim results*, *an interim dividend*.

> [!example] In a sentence
> - The board named the finance chief **interim** CEO while it searched for a permanent replacement.
> - The company's **interim** results showed profits up eight percent in the first half.
> - The new bridge opens in March; in the **interim**, a ferry will carry the traffic.

> [!info] Register & connotation
> Formal to neutral; at home in business, government, and law. It means temporary by design, until the real thing arrives. It is more official than *temporary* and less makeshift than *stopgap*. *Provisional* stresses that something may still change, and *acting* is used for a person filling a role (*acting director*). From Latin *interim*, "meanwhile", from *inter*, "between".
