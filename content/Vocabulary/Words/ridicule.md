# ridicule

> [!definition] /ˈrɪdɪkjuːl/ · *noun (also verb)*
> Mockery; words or behaviour intended to make someone or something look foolish. As a verb, to mock or deride. (You met it as **ridiculed**.)

> [!example] In a sentence
> - His proposal was met with open **ridicule**, and he never raised it again.
> - The cartoonists **ridiculed** the minister mercilessly all that summer.
> - She bore the **ridicule** of her classmates in stubborn silence.

> [!info] Register & connotation
> Neutral-register but cutting, and usually unkind — *ridicule* aims to humiliate by laughter, not to amuse. Stronger and more public than teasing. Related to the adjective *ludicrous* (deserving such mockery) by the same Latin root, *ridere*, to laugh.
