# reticence

> [!definition] /ˈrɛtɪsəns/ · *noun*
> Reluctance to reveal one's thoughts, feelings, or personal affairs; reserve in what one says. The adjective is *reticent*.

> [!example] In a sentence
> - His **reticence** about his past made people wonder what he was hiding.
> - She overcame her natural **reticence** and spoke at the meeting.
> - The minister's **reticence** on the subject only fed the rumours.

> [!info] Register & connotation
> Formal, and neutral to slightly positive: it suggests discretion or shyness rather than deceit. It is about holding back what you *say*, not shyness in general. *Reticent about* something is standard; *reticent to do* something, meaning "reluctant to", is common, but many usage guides count it a mistake, so prefer *reluctant to*.
