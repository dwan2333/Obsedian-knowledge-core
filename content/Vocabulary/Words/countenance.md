# countenance

> [!definition] /ˈkaʊntənəns/ · *noun (also verb)*
> A person's face, especially as it shows their feelings — their expression. As a verb, to give approval to or tolerate something (*she would not countenance a refusal*).

> [!example] In a sentence
> - A shadow crossed his **countenance** at the mention of her name.
> - She had an open, honest **countenance** that strangers trusted at once.
> - The committee would not **countenance** any change to the rules.

> [!info] Register & connotation
> Formal and literary in the "face" sense — grander and more expressive than "face," foregrounding what the features *reveal*. The verb ("to countenance") is also formal and most often used in the negative (*will not countenance*). A favourite of nineteenth-century prose.
