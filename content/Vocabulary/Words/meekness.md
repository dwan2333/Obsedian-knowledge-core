# meekness

> [!definition] /ˈmiːknəs/ · *noun*
> Gentleness and submissiveness; a quiet, uncomplaining readiness to yield rather than assert oneself. Can be a virtue (humility) or a fault (spinelessness), depending on the speaker.

> [!example] In a sentence
> - She bore every insult with a **meekness** that only sharpened his contempt.
> - There was a steely resolve beneath his outward **meekness**.
> - "Blessed are the meek" makes a virtue of the very **meekness** the world tends to trample.

> [!info] Register & connotation
> Formal and literary, with a faint biblical colouring. Softer and more inward than *submissiveness*; choose it when you want to suggest a temperament of gentle yielding rather than a single act of giving in.
