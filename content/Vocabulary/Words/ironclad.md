# ironclad

> [!definition] /ˈaɪərnklad/ · *adjective*
> So firm that it cannot be broken, altered, questioned, or escaped — said of a contract, guarantee, rule, argument, or alibi. (Also a noun, historically: a 19th-century warship clad in iron armour.)

> [!example] In a sentence
> - The lawyers drafted an **ironclad** contract that left neither side any room to wriggle out.
> - She had an **ironclad** alibi — three coworkers and a timestamped receipt put her across town.
> - His reasoning seemed **ironclad** until a single overlooked exception brought the whole case down.

> [!info] Register & connotation
> Neutral and emphatic; equally at home in everyday speech and in legal or business talk. Stronger than *firm*, *solid*, or *binding* — it claims there is simply no way through or around. Reach for it when you want to stress total, unbreakable certainty.
