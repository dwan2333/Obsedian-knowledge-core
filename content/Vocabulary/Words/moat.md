# moat

> [!definition] /məʊt/ (MOHT) · *noun*
> A deep, wide ditch around a castle or fort, usually filled with water, to keep attackers out. In business and investing, a lasting competitive advantage that protects a company's profits from rivals (*an economic moat*).

> [!example] In a sentence
> - Visitors crossed the old drawbridge over the castle's **moat**.
> - Warren Buffett looks for companies with a wide economic **moat**, such as a brand that rivals can't copy.
> - Network effects give the platform a **moat**: every new user makes it harder for a competitor to catch up.

> [!info] Register & connotation
> Neutral. The literal sense belongs to castles and history; the figurative sense is everyday investing jargon, made popular by Warren Buffett (*wide moat*, *narrow moat*, *no moat*). Moats in business come from brands, network effects, switching costs, patents, or low costs. From Old French *mote*, "mound, embankment".
