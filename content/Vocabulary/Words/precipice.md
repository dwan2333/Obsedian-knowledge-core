# precipice

> [!definition] /ˈprɛsɪpɪs/ · *noun*
> A very steep or sheer rock face; a cliff edge with a long drop. Figuratively, the brink of a dangerous or disastrous situation — *on the precipice of war*.

> [!example] In a sentence
> - The path narrowed until it ran along the very lip of a **precipice**, with the valley floor a thousand feet below.
> - By autumn the company was teetering on the **precipice** of bankruptcy.
> - One more scandal would push the government over the **precipice**.

> [!info] Register & connotation
> Neutral in its literal sense, but dramatic and ominous when used figuratively — and the figurative use is now the more common. Reach for it when "edge" or "brink" needs more height and menace.
