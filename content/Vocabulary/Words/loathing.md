# loathing

> [!definition] /ˈləʊðɪŋ/ (LOH-thing, with the *th* of *this*) · *noun*
> Intense dislike mixed with disgust; hatred. The verb is *loathe*, to hate something intensely. Don't confuse it with the adjective *loath* (/ləʊθ/, with the *th* of *thin*), which means unwilling: *loath to admit it*.

> [!example] In a sentence
> - She couldn't hide her **loathing** for the man who had cheated her father out of his savings.
> - He has a deep **loathing** of meetings that could have been an email.
> - The narrator's self-**loathing** colours every page: he despises everything he does, even his kindnesses.

> [!info] Register & connotation
> Strong and slightly literary, though common in writing of every kind. Stronger than *dislike* and close to *hatred*, but with a physical edge of disgust: you recoil from what you loathe. Usual patterns: *loathing for* or *of* something, *self-loathing*, and the pairing *fear and loathing*. From Old English *lāþ*, "hateful, hostile", the root of both *loathe* and *loath*.
