# inducement

> [!definition] /ɪnˈdjuːsmənt/ (in-DYOOS-ment) · *noun*
> Something that persuades or encourages someone to do something; an incentive. In legal and political contexts it can be a polite word for a bribe. (You met it as **inducements**.)

> [!example] In a sentence
> - The city offered ten years of tax breaks as an **inducement** for the company to build its factory there.
> - Free shipping on every order is a powerful **inducement** to keep shopping.
> - The official was charged with accepting illegal **inducements** from contractors.

> [!info] Register & connotation
> Formal; at home in business, economics, law, and policy writing. Neutral in the "incentive" sense, but in legal or political news it often hints at something improper: *financial inducements* to a regulator are bribes. In conversation, *incentive*, *perk*, or *sweetener* say it more plainly. From Latin *inducere*, "to lead in" (*in-* + *ducere* "to lead"), the root of *induce*, *conduct*, and *produce*.
