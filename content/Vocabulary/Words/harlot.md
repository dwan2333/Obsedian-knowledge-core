# harlot

> [!definition] /ˈhɑːlət/ · *noun*
> A prostitute; (archaic) a promiscuous or "fallen" woman. A dated, literary, and morally loaded term.

> [!example] In a sentence
> - The novel's great scandal was its tenderness toward a **harlot** the respectable world had cast out.
> - Preachers thundered against the **harlots** of the dockside, and quietly visited them.
> - She would rather starve, she said, than be taken for a **harlot**.

> [!info] Register & connotation
> Archaic, literary, and heavily judgmental — a word of the pulpit and the nineteenth-century novel, not of modern neutral speech. Carries centuries of moral condemnation, so use it only with awareness of that weight (often, as in Dostoevsky, ironically or compassionately).
