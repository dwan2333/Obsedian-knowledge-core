# idiosyncratic

> [!definition] /ˌɪdiəsɪŋˈkrætɪk/ (id-ee-oh-sin-KRAT-ik) · *adjective*
> Peculiar to one particular person or thing; individual and unusual in a way that sets it apart. Noun: **idiosyncrasy**, a personal quirk. In finance and statistics, specific to one company or asset rather than shared by the whole market: *idiosyncratic risk*.

> [!example] In a sentence
> - Her **idiosyncratic** filing system made sense to nobody else, yet she could find any paper in seconds.
> - The director's **idiosyncratic** style, all long silences and odd camera angles, divides audiences.
> - Holding many stocks removes most **idiosyncratic** risk but leaves market risk untouched.

> [!info] Register & connotation
> Formal to neutral, and not an insult: it calls something distinctively personal, sometimes fondly and sometimes as a polite way of saying odd. *Quirky* is the casual cousin, *eccentric* is stronger, and *peculiar* can sound critical. In finance and statistics it is a technical term, the opposite of *systematic*: an idiosyncratic shock hits one firm, a systematic one hits them all. From Greek *idios*, "one's own, private", plus *synkrasis*, "a mixture": a person's own particular blend. The same *idio-* is in *idiom*.
