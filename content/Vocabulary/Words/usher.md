# usher

> [!definition] /ˈʌʃə/ (USH-er) · *verb* · *noun*
> **Verb:** to show or guide someone to a place (*ushered us to our seats*). **Usher in:** to mark or cause the start of something new (*usher in a new era*). **Noun:** a person who shows people to their seats at a theatre, cinema, or wedding. (You met it as **ushering**.)

> [!example] In a sentence
> - A waiter **ushered** us to a quiet table at the back of the restaurant.
> - The steam engine **ushered in** the Industrial Revolution.
> - Cheap smartphones are **ushering in** a new era of mobile banking across Africa.

> [!info] Register & connotation
> Neutral. The literal sense is everyday and a little courteous: you usher a guest, politely leading them. The figurative *usher in* is a favourite of history and news writing, for a change that opens a new period. Plainer options are *show* or *lead* (literal) and *bring in*, *launch*, or *start* (figurative). From Old French *huissier*, "doorkeeper", from *huis*, "door": an usher stands at the door and lets the new thing in.
