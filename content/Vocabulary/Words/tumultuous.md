# tumultuous

> [!definition] /tjuːˈmʌltʃuəs/ (tyoo-MUL-choo-us) · *adjective*
> Full of noise, disorder, and upheaval. Of a period or a relationship: turbulent, with sudden changes and conflict (*a tumultuous year*). Of a crowd or its applause: loud and wildly excited (*tumultuous applause*). Noun: **tumult**.

> [!example] In a sentence
> - After a **tumultuous** year of strikes, resignations, and a failed merger, the company finally posted a profit.
> - The winning goal was met with **tumultuous** applause that shook the stadium.
> - Their **tumultuous** marriage ended and began again three times.

> [!info] Register & connotation
> Formal to neutral, and vivid. For a period or a relationship it leans negative, like *turbulent* or *stormy*. For applause or a welcome it is positive: wildly enthusiastic. *Chaotic* stresses disorder without the noise and energy. Its usual partners are *year, period, times, events, relationship,* and *applause, welcome, reception*. From Latin *tumultus*, "uproar, commotion", related to *tumere*, "to swell".
