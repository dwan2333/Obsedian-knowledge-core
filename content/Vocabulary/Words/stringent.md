# stringent

> [!definition] /ˈstrɪndʒənt/ (STRIN-junt) · *adjective*
> (Of rules, requirements, standards, or conditions) strict, precise, and demanding; leaving little room for exceptions. In finance, also tight: *stringent credit conditions*.

> [!example] In a sentence
> - The new drug had to pass **stringent** safety tests before it could be sold.
> - Banks now face more **stringent** capital requirements than they did before the crisis.
> - The school's admission standards are so **stringent** that fewer than one applicant in ten gets in.

> [!info] Register & connotation
> Formal; common in law, regulation, science, and finance news. Neutral to approving. It describes rules and standards, not people: a *stringent* rule, but a *strict* or stern teacher. Its usual partners are *rules, regulations, requirements, standards, controls, tests, criteria,* and *measures*. Plainer options are *strict*, *tough*, and *tight*. From Latin *stringere*, "to draw tight, to bind", the root of *strict*, *strain*, *constrict*, and *astringent*.
