# ensconce

> [!definition] /ɪnˈskɒns/ (en-SKONSS) · *verb*
> To settle someone, or yourself, firmly in a comfortable, safe, or hidden place. Mostly used as *ensconced*: *ensconced in an armchair*; figuratively, *firmly ensconced in power*. (You met it as **ensconced**.)

> [!example] In a sentence
> - By nine o'clock he was **ensconced** in his favourite armchair with a book and a pot of tea.
> - She **ensconced** herself in a quiet corner of the library where no one would bother her.
> - After twenty years in office, the senator was firmly **ensconced** in power.

> [!info] Register & connotation
> Slightly formal and literary, often with a playful, cosy tone when it's about comfort. In the figurative sense (*ensconced in a job*, *in power*) it means securely established and hard to dislodge, sometimes with a hint of complacency. Plainer options are *settled in*, *tucked away*, and, for positions, *entrenched*. From *sconce*, an old word for a small fort or earthwork shelter (Dutch *schans*): to ensconce is to put inside your own little fortress.
