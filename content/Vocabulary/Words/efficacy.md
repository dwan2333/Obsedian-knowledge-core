# efficacy

> [!definition] /ˈɛfɪkəsi/ · *noun*
> The power to produce the intended result; how well something works when it is put to the test. Said of treatments, methods, policies, and tools, and not usually of people.

> [!example] In a sentence
> - The trial tested the vaccine's **efficacy** in adults over sixty-five.
> - Economists still argue about the **efficacy** of sanctions as a way to change a government's behaviour.
> - She doubted the **efficacy** of shouting at a teenager, and tried silence instead.

> [!info] Register & connotation
> Formal and neutral, at home in medicine, science, and policy writing; in plain speech you would ask "does it work?". Keep it apart from two neighbours. *Efficiency* is about doing something with little waste, while *efficacy* is about whether it does the job at all. In medicine, *efficacy* is how well a treatment works in a controlled trial, and *effectiveness* is how well it works in ordinary practice. The adjective is *efficacious*.
