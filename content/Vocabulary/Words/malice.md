# malice

> [!definition] /ˈmælɪs/ · *noun*
> The desire to harm, injure, or cause suffering to another; active ill will. In law: the intent to do wrong, or reckless disregard for it, as distinct from accident.

> [!example] In a sentence
> - There was no **malice** in the remark — she simply hadn't thought how it would land.
> - He spoke of his rivals without bitterness, entirely free of **malice**.
> - The court had to decide whether the act was negligence or **malice** aforethought.

> [!info] Register & connotation
> Formal and literary, strongly negative. Heavier and colder than *spite*, which is petty; malice suggests a settled, deliberate wish to hurt. The phrase *malice aforethought* is a legal term of art for premeditated intent.
