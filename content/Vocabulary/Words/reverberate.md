# reverberate

> [!definition] /rɪˈvɜːbəreɪt/ (rih-VUR-buh-rayt) · *verb*
> (Of a loud sound) to echo again and again, filling a space. Figuratively, (of an event or a piece of news) to have strong effects that spread widely and last. Noun: **reverberation**. (You met it as **reverberated**.)

> [!example] In a sentence
> - The gunshot **reverberated** through the empty warehouse.
> - The bank's collapse **reverberated** through financial markets for months.
> - Her resignation speech still **reverberates** in the party today.

> [!info] Register & connotation
> Neutral to literary for sound. In news the figurative sense is common: shocks, scandals, and decisions *reverberate through*, *across*, or *around* a market, an industry, or a country. It suggests a deep, repeated, far-reaching effect: stronger than *echo*, which can be faint. From Latin *reverberare*, "to beat back" (*re-* "back" + *verberare* "to lash, to beat").
