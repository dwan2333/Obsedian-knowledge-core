# juncture

> [!definition] /ˈdʒʌŋ(k)tʃə(r)/ (JUNK-cher) · *noun*
> A particular point in time or in a series of events, especially a critical one, when a decision or change is due (*at this juncture; a critical juncture*). Less often, a place where two things join: a junction or joint.

> [!example] In a sentence
> - At this **juncture**, the negotiators could either sign the deal or walk away for good.
> - Historians treat the summer of 1914 as a critical **juncture**: choices made in a few weeks shaped the whole century.
> - "I'd rather not comment at this **juncture**," the spokesperson said, which everyone understood to mean "not yet."

> [!info] Register & connotation
> Formal and neutral. At home in political, diplomatic, business, and historical writing. *At this juncture* is a stock formal phrase in meetings and press briefings, and can sound stiff where *now* or *at this point* would do. Add *critical, crucial, pivotal,* or *historic* when you mean a turning point. Where *moment* is just a point in time, a *juncture* is a point where things meet and something has to give. From Latin *junctura*, "a joining", from *jungere*, "to join"; the same root as *junction*, *conjunction*, and *joint*.
