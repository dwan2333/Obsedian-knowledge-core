# dissipated

> [!definition] /ˈdɪsɪpeɪtɪd/ · *adjective*
> Of a person: worn down by self-indulgence, given over to drink, pleasure, and dissolute living, often with the marks of it showing. The related verb *dissipate* means to fritter away or squander (energy, money, an inheritance).

> [!example] In a sentence
> - He had the **dissipated** look of a man who slept little and drank a great deal.
> - Years of late nights had left her once-handsome brother pale and **dissipated**.
> - The novel follows a charming but **dissipated** aristocrat as his fortune drains away.

> [!info] Register & connotation
> Formal and literary, clearly negative — it suggests not just indulgence but its visible toll. Choose it over *wild* when you mean a life of pleasure that has begun to corrode the person living it.
