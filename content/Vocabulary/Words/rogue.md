# rogue

> [!definition] /rəʊɡ/ · *noun*
> A dishonest or unprincipled person; a scoundrel. Often used more affectionately of a mischievous, roguish rascal. (As an adjective, behaving unpredictably or outside normal control — *a rogue trader*.)

> [!example] In a sentence
> - The old **rogue** had cheated half the village and somehow remained everyone's favourite.
> - "You charming **rogue**," she laughed, half-scolding, as he pocketed the last sweet.
> - A single **rogue** clerk had been falsifying the accounts for years.

> [!info] Register & connotation
> Everyday and flexible — it swings from genuine condemnation (a scoundrel) to warm teasing (a lovable rascal), and tone decides which. Lighter and more charming than "criminal"; a rogue has a twinkle even when he shouldn't.
