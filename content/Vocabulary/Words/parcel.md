# parcel

> [!definition] /ˈpɑːsəl/ (UK), /ˈpɑːrsəl/ (US) · *noun* · *verb*
> *Noun:* (1) something wrapped up to be carried or sent by post; a package. (2) A piece of land, usually part of a larger area (*a parcel of land*). (3) A portion or group of something (*a parcel of shares*). *Verb:* **parcel out**, to divide something into portions and hand them out; **parcel up**, to wrap something into a parcel.

> [!example] In a sentence
> - The **parcel** arrived two days late, with one corner crushed.
> - The developer bought three **parcels** of land along the river.
> - Grandmother's jewellery was **parcelled out** among the five grandchildren.
> - Long hours are **part and parcel** of the job. (*part and parcel of*: an essential part of)

> [!info] Register & connotation
> Everyday in British English for something sent by post; Americans usually say *package*. *Parcel of land* is the standard term in property and legal documents. *Parcel out* is plainer than *apportion* and does not suggest the shares are fair. British spelling doubles the *l* (*parcelled*, *parcelling*); American usually does not (*parceled*).
