# profligate

> [!definition] /ˈprɒflɪɡət/ · *adjective (also noun)*
> Recklessly extravagant or wasteful, especially with money or resources. As a noun, a person who lives this way — a spendthrift given to dissolute habits. By extension, *profligate* can describe wastefulness of any kind (*profligate use of water*).

> [!example] In a sentence
> - The government was accused of **profligate** spending that left the treasury empty.
> - A **profligate** in his youth, he died without a penny of the great fortune he had inherited.
> - Such **profligate** consumption of energy cannot go on indefinitely.

> [!info] Register & connotation
> Formal, strongly negative — it condemns both the waste and, often, the moral looseness behind it. Sharper and more censorious than *extravagant*: use it when the spending is not just lavish but ruinous.
