# abject

> [!definition] /ˈæbdʒɛkt/ · *adjective*
> (Of a state or condition) utterly hopeless and wretched, sunk to the lowest degree — *abject poverty, abject failure*. (Of a person or behaviour) cringing and servile, stripped of all dignity — *an abject apology*.

> [!example] In a sentence
> - They lived in **abject** poverty, the kind that strips away every comfort and most of one's pride.
> - His **abject** apology — all grovelling and no substance — somehow made the insult worse.
> - The once-proud general was reduced to **abject** submission before the new regime.
> - His abject failure had result his 

> [!info] Register & connotation
> Formal, literary, and strongly negative. Reach for it when "bad" or "humble" is far too mild: *abject* marks the absolute bottom, whether of misery or of self-debasement. Applied to a person, it carries a note of contempt.
