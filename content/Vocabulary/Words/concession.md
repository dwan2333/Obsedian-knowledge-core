# concession

> [!definition] /kənˈsɛʃən/ (kun-SESH-un) · *noun*
> Something given up or granted to the other side, especially to reach an agreement; also the act of admitting that a point is true. Other senses: a reduced price for a group such as students (*concessions available*), and a right granted to run a business on someone else's property (*a mining concession*, *a concession stand*). (You met it as **concessions**.)

> [!example] In a sentence
> - Neither side would make **concessions**, so the trade talks collapsed after two days.
> - As a **concession** to the striking workers, the company agreed to cap overtime at ten hours a week.
> - In her **concession** speech, the losing candidate congratulated her opponent and asked her supporters to accept the result.

> [!info] Register & connotation
> Neutral and common in news about politics, labour disputes, trade talks, and elections. A concession is ground you give up, so it implies the other side had leverage; *make concessions* and *wring concessions from* are the usual pairings. In conversation, *giving ground* or *meeting halfway* say it more plainly. From Latin *concedere*, "to yield, give way" (*con-* + *cedere* "to go, yield"), the root of *concede*, *recede*, and *access*.
