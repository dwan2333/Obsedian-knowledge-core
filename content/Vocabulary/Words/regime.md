# regime

> [!definition] /reɪˈʒiːm/ (ray-ZHEEM) · *noun*
> A government, especially an authoritarian one or one the speaker disapproves of (*the military regime*). Also a system of rules for how something is run (*a tax regime*, *a regulatory regime*), and a planned routine of exercise, diet, or treatment (*a fitness regime*).

> [!example] In a sentence
> - The **regime** jailed journalists who questioned the election result.
> - Under the new tax **regime**, small firms pay a flat rate on profits.
> - Her training **regime** starts at five every morning, six days a week.

> [!info] Register & connotation
> In the government sense it carries disapproval: calling a government a *regime* implies that it is illegitimate or oppressive, while friendly ones are *governments* or *administrations*. The other two senses are neutral. Common news phrases are *regime change*, *sanctions regime*, and *regulatory regime*. For a health routine, *regimen* is the more precise word, especially in American English. From French *régime*, from Latin *regimen*, "rule, guidance", from *regere*, "to rule", the root of *regulate* and *regent*.
