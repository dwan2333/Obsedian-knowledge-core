# cede

> [!definition] /siːd/ (SEED) · *verb*
> To give up power, territory, control, or a right to someone else, usually formally and under pressure — by treaty, defeat, or negotiation. Also, more loosely, to give up ground or a lead (*cede the advantage*). (You met it as **ceding**.)

> [!example] In a sentence
> - After the war, the kingdom **ceded** two border provinces to its neighbour under the peace treaty.
> - The founder refused to **cede** control of the company, even after investors took a majority stake.
> - The champion **ceded** the lead on the final lap and never won it back.

> [!info] Register & connotation
> Formal; at home in history, diplomacy, law, and business news (*cede territory, cede control, cede power, cede ground*). Neutral in tone, but it implies reluctance: you cede what you would rather keep. The pattern is *cede something to someone*. Don't confuse it with *concede* (to admit a point or a defeat) or *secede* (to withdraw from a union). Plainer options are *give up*, *hand over*, and *surrender*. From Latin *cedere*, "to go, yield", the root of *concession*, *concede*, *recede*, and *precede*.
