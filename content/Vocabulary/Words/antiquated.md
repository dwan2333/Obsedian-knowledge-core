# antiquated

> [!definition] /ˈæntɪkweɪtɪd/ (AN-tih-kway-tid) · *adjective*
> Old-fashioned or outdated; kept on past the point of being useful or suitable — said of ideas, laws, methods, and equipment.

> [!example] In a sentence
> - The bank still ran on an **antiquated** computer system that only two engineers knew how to repair.
> - Harris's account of floor trading reads as **antiquated** now that most orders are matched by computers.
> - Critics called the dress code **antiquated** and out of step with how people actually work.

> [!info] Register & connotation
> Neutral-to-negative and moderately formal; calling something antiquated is a criticism — it should have been replaced. Compare *antique* (valued *because* it is old) and *vintage* (approving). In conversation, *outdated* or *old-fashioned* say the same thing more plainly. From Latin *antiquare*, "to make old; to reject (a law)", from *antiquus*, "ancient".
