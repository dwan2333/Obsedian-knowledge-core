# dilapidated

> [!definition] /dɪˈlæpɪdeɪtɪd/ (dih-LAP-ih-day-tid) · *adjective*
> (Of a building, vehicle, or object) fallen into disrepair or partial ruin through age and neglect. Figuratively, of institutions or services that have been left to run down.

> [!example] In a sentence
> - They bought a **dilapidated** farmhouse with a sagging roof and spent two years restoring it.
> - A **dilapidated** bus rattled up the mountain road, its windows held shut with tape.
> - Critics blamed years of underfunding for the city's **dilapidated** public hospitals.

> [!info] Register & connotation
> Neutral and descriptive; common in both writing and speech, and mildly negative because it implies neglect, not only age. It stresses physical decay: *run-down* and *falling apart* are the everyday equivalents, *ramshackle* adds a sense of rickety construction. From Latin *dilapidare*, "to scatter, squander", literally "to throw the stones apart" (*lapis, lapid-* "stone"), so it began as a word for stone buildings coming apart.
