# circumvent

> [!definition] /ˌsɜːrkəmˈvɛnt/ · *verb*
> To find a clever way around an obstacle, rule, or difficulty; to bypass or evade it rather than confront it head-on. (You met it as **circumvented**.)

> [!example] In a sentence
> - The company **circumvented** the import ban by routing goods through a third country.
> - A good interface lets users **circumvent** the tedious steps without breaking anything.
> - They **circumvented** the awkward question by changing the subject entirely.

> [!info] Register & connotation
> Formal and neutral-to-slightly-disapproving — circumventing a *problem* is clever; circumventing a *rule or law* hints at sly evasion. From Latin *circum-* (around) + *venire* (to come): literally "to come around."
