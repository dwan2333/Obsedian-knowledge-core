# fret

> [!definition] /frɛt/ (FRET) · *verb*
> To worry in a restless, nagging way, often about small things or things you can't control. As a noun, a state of such worry (*in a fret*). (You met it as **fretted**.) The *frets* on a guitar's neck are an unrelated word.

> [!example] In a sentence
> - She **fretted** all night about whether she had locked the front door.
> - Don't **fret** over one bad quiz score; it's the trend across the week that matters.
> - Investors **fretted** that the central bank would raise rates again before the summer.

> [!info] Register & connotation
> Neutral and everyday, a little old-fashioned in speech (*don't fret*), and common in news (*investors fret*). It describes worry that keeps going round in circles, more fidgety than deep: you *fret about* or *fret over* something. Stronger words for fear are [apprehension](apprehension.md) and *dread*. From Old English *fretan*, "to devour, eat away", so fretting is worry that eats at you.
