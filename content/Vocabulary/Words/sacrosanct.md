# sacrosanct

> [!definition] /ˈsækrəʊsæŋkt/ (SAK-roh-sankt) · *adjective*
> Treated as too important or too precious to be changed, questioned, or interfered with. Literally, sacred and inviolable.

> [!example] In a sentence
> - In their house, Sunday dinner is **sacrosanct**: phones off, everyone at the table.
> - The founder treated the original design as **sacrosanct** and refused to change a single line of it.
> - No budget line should be **sacrosanct** when the company is losing money every quarter.

> [!info] Register & connotation
> Formal. Today it is mostly secular and often a little ironic or critical: calling a rule *sacrosanct* can mean people protect it more than it deserves (*nothing is sacrosanct*, *treated as sacrosanct*). Plainer options are *untouchable*, *off-limits*, and *sacred*. From Latin *sacrosanctus*, "made sacred by a religious rite" (*sacer* "sacred" + *sanctus* "holy").
