# parlance

> [!definition] /ˈpɑːləns/ (PAR-lunss) · *noun*
> A particular way of speaking or using words, especially the vocabulary shared by a group, field, or profession.

> [!example] In a sentence
> - In Wall Street **parlance**, a "haircut" is a cut in the value of collateral, not a trip to the barber.
> - What gamers call "nerfing" means, in common **parlance**, simply making something weaker.
> - In military **parlance**, a retreat may be called a "strategic withdrawal".

> [!info] Register & connotation
> Slightly formal and neutral, and almost always found in two fixed patterns: *in [field] parlance* (*legal*, *medical*, *Silicon Valley*) and *in common parlance*. Reach for it when you're about to translate a group's jargon for outsiders. Plainer options are *jargon*, *lingo*, and *way of speaking*. From Old French *parler*, "to speak", the root shared with *parliament*, *parley*, and *parlour* (once a room for talking).
