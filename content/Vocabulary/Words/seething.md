# seething

> [!definition] /ˈsiːðɪŋ/ · *adjective*
> Boiling with barely controlled anger or agitation. (Literally, of a liquid, churning and bubbling; of a crowd, teeming and turbulent.)

> [!example] In a sentence
> - He said nothing, but she could see he was **seething**.
> - By midday the square was a **seething** mass of protesters.
> - Beneath the polite reply lay a **seething** resentment years in the making.

> [!info] Register & connotation
> Literary-leaning and vivid, negative — it pictures anger as a barely-contained boil, hotter and more suppressed than open rage. Also used neutrally of churning liquid or teeming crowds. The image is always of turbulence beneath a surface.
