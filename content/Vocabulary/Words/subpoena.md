# subpoena

> [!definition] /səˈpiːnə/ (suh-PEE-nuh) · *noun (also verb)*
> A written legal order requiring a person to appear in court or before an inquiry to give evidence, or to hand over documents. As a verb, to issue such an order to someone. (You met it as **subpoenas**.)

> [!example] In a sentence
> - The committee issued **subpoenas** to three former executives who had refused to testify.
> - Prosecutors **subpoenaed** the bank's records going back ten years.
> - Ignoring a **subpoena** can land you in jail for contempt of court.

> [!info] Register & connotation
> A formal legal term, neutral in tone; chiefly American law and politics (British courts issue a *witness summons*). The *b* and the *o* are silent, and the spelling is *sub* plus *poena*. Its usual partners are *issue, serve, comply with,* and *defy*; the past tense is *subpoenaed*. From Latin *sub poena*, "under penalty", the opening words of the order, which warned what would follow if the person stayed away. *Poena* is also the root of *penalty*, *penal*, and *punish*.
