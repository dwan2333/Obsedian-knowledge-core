# saga

> [!definition] /ˈsɑːɡə/ (SAH-guh) · *noun*
> A long, complicated series of events, often one that drags on with many twists (*the takeover saga*). Originally a long medieval Norse or Icelandic prose story of heroes and families; also a long story that spans generations (*a family saga*).

> [!example] In a sentence
> - The takeover **saga** dragged on for eighteen months before regulators finally approved the deal.
> - Getting the visa turned into a **saga** of lost forms, wrong stamps, and three trips to the embassy.
> - The novel is a family **saga** that follows four generations through war and exile.

> [!info] Register & connotation
> Neutral. In news it is a favourite label for a long-running affair (*the transfer saga*, *the budget saga*), with a hint that it has gone on too long. In everyday speech it is lightly humorous for any drawn-out ordeal. The literary sense is neutral to grand. From Old Norse *saga*, "a story, something said", related to *say*. It is unrelated to *sagacious*, which comes from Latin *sagax*, "wise".
