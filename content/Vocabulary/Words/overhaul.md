# overhaul

> [!definition] /ˌəʊvəˈhɔːl/ (oh-ver-HAWL) · *verb* · /ˈəʊvəhɔːl/ (OH-ver-hawl) · *noun*
> **Verb:** to take something apart, examine it, and repair or rebuild it thoroughly; to revise or reform a system completely. **Noun:** a thorough examination and repair; a complete reform. The stress moves: the verb is stressed on *-haul*, the noun on *o-*.

> [!example] In a sentence
> - The engine needs a complete **overhaul** before the boat goes back in the water.
> - The new government promised to **overhaul** the tax system within two years.
> - After the third bug in a week, he decided to **overhaul** the whole script instead of patching it again.

> [!info] Register & connotation
> Neutral and very common in news, business, and technical writing. It promises thoroughness: an overhaul goes deeper than a *fix* or an *update* and is planned, where [upend](upend.md) is sudden. Close to *revamp* (a bit more informal) and *reform* (for laws and institutions).
