# retrench

> [!definition] /rɪˈtrɛntʃ/ (rih-TRENCH) · *verb*
> To cut back spending or reduce the size of an operation, usually because money is tight; to scale down. In Singapore, Malaysia, South Africa, and Australia it also means to lay workers off (*he was retrenched*). Noun: **retrenchment**.

> [!example] In a sentence
> - When orders collapsed, the company had to **retrench**, closing two factories and freezing hiring.
> - Facing a budget deficit, the city **retrenched**, delaying the new metro line by five years.
> - Five hundred workers were **retrenched** when the plant shut down.

> [!info] Register & connotation
> Formal; at home in business news, economics, and government budgets. Neutral in tone but signals hardship: it's a defensive move made under pressure. Plainer options are *cut back*, *scale back*, and *downsize*. From French *retrencher*, "to cut off" (*re-* + *trencher* "to cut"), the root of *trench*. In old military use a retrenchment was a second line of defence dug behind the first, so to retrench is to fall back to a smaller line you can hold.
