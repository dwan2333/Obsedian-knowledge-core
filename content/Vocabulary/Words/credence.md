# credence

> [!definition] /ˈkriːdəns/ (KREE-dunss) · *noun*
> Belief in or acceptance of something as true. Mostly in two set phrases: *give credence to* (to believe, to treat as true) and *lend credence to* (to make more believable).

> [!example] In a sentence
> - The leaked emails lent **credence** to rumours that the merger was already agreed.
> - Few economists give much **credence** to forecasts made more than a year ahead.
> - The theory gained **credence** after a second lab repeated the result.

> [!info] Register & connotation
> Formal and neutral; common in journalism, law, and academic writing. It lives almost entirely in phrases: *give, lend,* or *add credence to*, and *gain credence*. The two main phrases point in different directions: a person *gives* credence (believes), while evidence *lends* credence (supports). Don't confuse it with *credibility*, the quality of being believable (a witness has credibility). Plainer words are *belief*, *support*, and *weight*. From Latin *credere*, "to believe", the root of *credit*, *credible*, *creed*, and *incredible*.
