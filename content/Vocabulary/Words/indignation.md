# indignation

> [!definition] /ˌɪndɪɡˈneɪʃən/ · *noun*
> Anger or scorn aroused by something felt to be unjust, mean, or unworthy; righteous displeasure.

> [!example] In a sentence
> - She rose from her seat, trembling with **indignation** at the insult.
> - The verdict was met with public **indignation** and a week of furious headlines.
> - "And you call that fair?" he cried, his **indignation** getting the better of him.

> [!info] Register & connotation
> Formal and literary, but widely understood. Distinctly *moral* anger — it carries a claim that a line has been crossed, which plain *anger* does not. The adjective is *indignant*. Reach for it when the feeling is principled, not merely hot-tempered.
