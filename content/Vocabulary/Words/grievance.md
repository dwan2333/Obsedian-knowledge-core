# grievance

> [!definition] /ˈɡriːvəns/ · *noun*
> A real or imagined wrong that gives a person grounds for complaint, especially a lasting sense of having been unfairly treated. Also: the formal complaint itself, as lodged in a workplace or institution.

> [!example] In a sentence
> - Years later he still nursed his **grievance**, recounting the slight to anyone who would listen.
> - The strike grew out of a long list of **grievances** over pay, hours, and unsafe conditions.
> - She filed a formal **grievance** with the union the morning after she was passed over.

> [!info] Register & connotation
> Neutral-to-formal; common in legal, political, and workplace contexts. Unlike a passing *complaint*, a grievance implies a standing wrong that festers — reach for it when the resentment has weight and history behind it.
