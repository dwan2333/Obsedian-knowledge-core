# mired

> [!definition] /maɪəd/ (UK), /maɪrd/ (US) · *adjective (past participle of* mire*)*
> Stuck fast in mud or a bog. Figuratively, caught in a difficult situation that is hard to get out of; nearly always with *in* — *mired in debt*, *mired in scandal*, *mired in recession*. The noun **mire** is swampy, boggy ground, or a messy situation.

> [!example] In a sentence
> - The truck was **mired** in mud up to its axles.
> - The project has been **mired** in delays and budget overruns for two years.
> - The country remains **mired** in a recession with no end in sight.

> [!info] Register & connotation
> Neutral to formal and very common in news writing; negative, with a sense of slow, stuck helplessness. *Embroiled* stresses being dragged into a dispute; *mired* stresses being stuck and unable to move forward. *Hampered* is only slowed down, where mired is stuck fast. *Bogged down* is the informal twin, built on the same mud image.
