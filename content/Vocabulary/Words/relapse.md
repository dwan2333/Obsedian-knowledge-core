# relapse

> [!definition] /rɪˈlæps/ · *verb* · /ˈriːlæps/ or /rɪˈlæps/ · *noun*
> *Verb:* to fall ill again after starting to get better, or to slip back into a bad habit or an earlier state you had left behind. Used with *into* — *relapse into drinking*, *relapse into silence*. *Noun:* a single time this happens.

> [!example] In a sentence
> - Three months after the surgery she **relapsed**, and the doctors started a second round of treatment.
> - He had been sober for two years before a **relapse** last winter.
> - After a burst of conversation, the table **relapsed** into an awkward silence.

> [!info] Register & connotation
> Neutral. It is the standard word in medicine and addiction recovery, and it is also used more generally for drifting back into an earlier state. A *lapse* is a single slip, while a *relapse* means going back to the whole old pattern: one cigarette after quitting is a lapse, a pack a day again is a relapse. *Backslide* is informal and moralizing, with religious roots. The opposites are *recovery* and *remission*.
