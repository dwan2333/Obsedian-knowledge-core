# untenable

> [!definition] /ʌnˈtɛnəbəl/ (un-TEN-uh-bul) · *adjective*
> (Of a position, argument, or situation) impossible to defend or to keep going; it can no longer be held.

> [!example] In a sentence
> - Once the emails leaked, the minister's position became **untenable**, and he resigned within a week.
> - The claim that prices can rise forever is simply **untenable**.
> - Paying rent on two flats was financially **untenable**, so they moved in together.

> [!info] Register & connotation
> Formal but common in news, politics, law, and debate; firmly negative. It names two things: an argument that can't be defended, and a situation that can't be sustained. Plainer options are *indefensible* (arguments) and *unsustainable* (situations). From Latin *tenere*, "to hold": the same root as *tenable*, *tenacious*, *tenant*, *tenure*, and *maintain*.
