# whisk

> [!definition] /wɪsk/ (WISK) · *verb (also noun)*
> To take or move someone or something somewhere quickly and suddenly, usually *whisked away* or *whisked off*. Also to beat eggs or cream with a quick, light motion; as a noun, the kitchen tool for doing that. (You met it as **whisked**.)

> [!example] In a sentence
> - The minister was **whisked** away by security before reporters could ask a single question.
> - A waiter **whisked** our plates off the table the moment we put down our forks.
> - **Whisk** the eggs and sugar until the mixture turns pale and thick.

> [!info] Register & connotation
> Everyday, vivid, and neutral. The movement sense nearly always takes *away* or *off* and is often passive (*was whisked to hospital*). It suggests speed and smooth efficiency, and often that the person being moved had little say in it. It is lighter than *rushed* and less forceful than *hustled* or *bundled*. From Old Norse *visk*, "a wisp, a small bundle of hay": the picture is a light, quick sweep.
