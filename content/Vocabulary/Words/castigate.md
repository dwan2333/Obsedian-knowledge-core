# castigate

> [!definition] /ˈkæstɪɡeɪt/ (KAS-tih-gayt) · *verb*
> To criticize or scold someone severely, especially in public. (You met it as **castigating**.)

> [!example] In a sentence
> - The judge **castigated** the bank for hiding the losses from its own auditors.
> - Opposition leaders **castigated** the minister over the delays.
> - She **castigated** herself for weeks over one careless remark.

> [!info] Register & connotation
> Formal; common in news and commentary. It is harsher than *criticize* or *rebuke*, with a sense of punishing through words. The pattern is *castigate someone for something* or *over something*. Plainer options are *slam*, *tear into*, and *tell off*; *lambast* and *excoriate* are close and just as strong. From Latin *castigare*, "to correct, to chastise", from *castus*, "pure", which also gives *chaste* and *chastise*: to castigate was first to make pure.
