# allay

> [!definition] /əˈleɪ/ (uh-LAY) · *verb*
> To reduce or put to rest a fear, suspicion, or worry; also, to ease a pain or a hunger. Almost always used with *fears*, *concerns*, *suspicions*, or *doubts* as its object.

> [!example] In a sentence
> - The central bank's statement did little to **allay** fears of another rate hike.
> - To **allay** his parents' concerns, he sent them his weekly study plan every Sunday.
> - A bowl of soup **allayed** her hunger until dinner.

> [!info] Register & connotation
> Formal and very common in news and official statements (*to allay fears*, *to allay concerns*). Positive in effect: it calms. Note that the worry is allayed, not the person; you allay someone's fears, never the person themselves. In conversation, *ease*, *calm*, or *put someone's mind at rest* say it more plainly. Don't confuse it with *ally* (a-LIE). From Old English *ālecgan*, "to lay down, put aside".
