# epoch

> [!definition] /ˈɛpək/ (US) · /ˈiːpɒk/ (UK) · *noun*
> A distinct and notable period in history, a life, or the development of something — usually one marked by particular events or characteristics. Also the *beginning* of such a period.

> [!example] In a sentence
> - The printing press opened a new **epoch** in the spread of ideas.
> - Geologists read the rock as a record of one **epoch** laid over another.
> - Her election marked an **epoch** in the country's political life.

> [!info] Register & connotation
> Formal and slightly grand, neutral in feeling. Larger and weightier than *period*; an *epoch* is a stretch of time significant enough to be named. Common in history, science, and reflective writing.
