# clamp

> [!definition] /klæmp/ (KLAMP) · *verb (also noun)*
> To fasten or hold something tightly in place, as with a clamp (the tool). In news use, mostly in the phrasal verb **clamp down (on)**: to take firm, sudden action to stop or limit something; the noun is **clampdown**. (You met it as **clamping**.)

> [!example] In a sentence
> - Regulators are **clamping** down on banks that hide risky loans from their balance sheets.
> - She **clamped** a hand over her mouth to keep from laughing.
> - The government's **clampdown** on street protests drew criticism from abroad.

> [!info] Register & connotation
> The literal sense is everyday and neutral (*clamp the board to the bench*, *clamp your jaw shut*). *Clamp down on* is journalistic and firm: authorities clamp down on crime, spending, dissent, or speculation. Whether it sounds good or bad depends on what is being stopped. *Crack down on* is its near twin and a little harsher; *curb* and *rein in* are milder. The pattern is *clamp down on something*. From Middle Dutch *klampe*, "a clasp, a brace".
